# Scenario C — Crew as a setting (D5)

**Design memo · Maritime Software-Defined Vessel · 17 October 2026**

**Implicit crew rule made explicit:** Never operate G2's winch while G3's submerged survey vehicle is listening. G3 requires quiet, a steady 3-knot course and no deck machinery. G2's winch is deck machinery. The governor treats simultaneous G2 `winch_operation` and G3 `surveying` as a hard operational conflict (Guest Cards G2/G3, Annex B). Mission priority chooses who waits; it does **not** waive the interlock. This rule is deterministic, independent of shore connectivity, and recorded with the reason and state transition times.

**Inputs/trigger.** A winch request arrives while G3 is surveying, or G3 requests a survey leg while G2's winch is active. Read `crew_count ∈ {14,6,0}`, `speed_kn`, `course`, `noise_level`, `deck_machinery_active`, active guest modes, mission-priority file, G3 charge obligation, and any G4 recovery deadline. The decision engine must check physical and safety constraints *before* maximizing mission utility. A lower-priority action is deferred or a currently interruptible operation is halted. Non-interruptible G3 charging is protected; an emergency G4 battery deadline requires a separate right-of-way decision. A permitted maneuver is not proof it can begin instantly: Annex A.6 gives 1–3 minutes to settle course, 2–4 minutes to go quiet, and 3–5 / 8–12 minutes to clear deck depending on crew.

| Crew setting | What a human confirms | What becomes automatic | What is impossible or held |
|---|---|---|---|
| **14 — patrol** | Contested but nonurgent reordering and physical work; the example planning assumption is confirmation within 15–60 s | Governor immediately blocks the forbidden concurrent winch/survey modes, schedules safe deferral, logs the binding rule and re-evaluates after state transitions | Concurrent winch + listening is always forbidden. Deck clearing takes approximately 3–5 min even with full crew |
| **6 — monitoring** | Confirms nonurgent contested changes where timing allows; confirmation may take 1–3 min | Same interlock and priority evaluation, with longer crew-dependent deck-clearing estimate (8–12 min) | No action may be planned as if crew were at full strength; missed deadlines cause alternative/defer/deny outcomes |
| **0 — night transit** | None is available | For clearly preauthorized logical mode changes, the governor enforces the interlock, defers lower-priority work and logs its rationale without shore dependence | Manual deck clearing, physical winch handling and component swaps cannot be newly authorized. If the deck is not already clear, reject a recovery requiring crew action; do not invent autonomous machinery authority |

**Response-time and audit rule.** D3 arbitration target: ≤2 s for the computation; D4 first action ≤5 s, full shedding sequence ≤60 s; safety commands ≤200 ms (Annex A.8). These limits refer to issuing decisions/commands, *not* waiting for a 1–12 min physical maneuver or human confirmation. Report command time separately from ship-state completion time. Record the initial state, mission-priority version, candidates, denied action, binding conflict, losing capability-minutes, next possible time and resulting ship state. A stored replay must yield the same decision for the same state and priorities.

**Example for the jury.** Under survey priority, keep G3 listening, defer G2's winch and report lost ocean-probe minutes. Under rescue priority when G4 has a hard landing deadline, preserve the emergency recovery window first, then reschedule both survey and winch as conditions permit. At zero crew, a plan requiring someone to clear or rig deck equipment is NOT-NOW or NO, even if electrical power and compute remain available. The prototype's D5 crew policy lives in `config/ops.yaml`, with its arbitration in `governor/rightofway.py`. This memo states the conservative operational policy; it does not certify human factors, unmanned-deck automation, or sensor/actuator timings.
